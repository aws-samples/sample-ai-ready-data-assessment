This dimension assesses whether an enterprise's investment in building and operating data capabilities for AI scenarios is **visible, quantifiable, explainable, and worth it**. It has three layers of meaning: first, whether AI-related data-side investment (e.g. acquisition, integration, management, governance) can be clearly identified and consolidated out of overall IT spending, avoiding unmanageable "hidden costs"; second, whether such investment is placed under measurable business outcomes—for example, whether it shortens the time to launch AI scenarios, reduces data preparation and rework, and improves the accuracy of key metrics and scenario stability; and third, whether major investment and adjustment decisions are grounded in explicit factual evidence and pre-agreed judgment criteria rather than relying mainly on individual experience, so that leadership has a basis for choosing among "keep investing, change direction, or pause."

## 1.1 Is Investment Visible

### Q1
**Q1. Can AI-related data-side investment be tracked and accounted for separately via a tagging system or a dedicated cost account?**

- A. Not at all—AI data-side investment is mixed into overall IT spending, cannot be identified separately, and there is no plan to establish a tracking mechanism
- B. Partially trackable—some costs (e.g. cloud resources) can be identified via tags, but indirect investments such as staff hours and tool licenses are not yet included in the tracking system
- C. Basically trackable—a dedicated cost account or tagging system has been established that can track most (ideally over 70%) AI data-side investment, covering direct and major indirect investments, though precision and timeliness still have room for improvement
- D. Systematically managed—there is a complete tagging system and cost account, all AI data-side investment (including staff, platform, external services) can be tracked in real time, and there is a fixed-cadence (e.g. quarterly) review of definition consistency with a review record kept
- N/A. Not applicable—the enterprise currently has no AI data-side investment to consolidate (e.g. still in the evaluation stage, no formal project yet)

### Q2
**Q2. When the same data asset is reused by multiple AI scenarios, has a corresponding cost-allocation mechanism been established (including staff-hour investment and platform running cost)?**

- A. No allocation—cost is attributed to the team that originally built it or to no one, and reuse scenarios "ride for free," leaving the input-output relationship unclear
- B. Rough estimate—there is a general sense of allocation, but it relies mainly on manual estimates or splitting evenly by number of scenarios, without accounting for differences in actual usage
- C. Rule-based allocation—an allocation rule based on usage volume or call frequency has been established, covering platform running cost, but the allocation of staff-hour investment is not yet precise and the rules do not cover all reuse scenarios
- D. Fine-grained allocation—there is a complete cost-allocation model covering staff hours, platform resources, storage and compute consumption; each scenario can view its own true investment, and the allocation coefficients are recalibrated on a fixed cadence
- N/A. Not applicable—the enterprise currently has no case of a data asset being reused by multiple AI scenarios

## 1.2 Is Value Measurable

### Q3
**Q3. Has the enterprise defined a value-measurement method for evaluating "data serving AI scenarios"?**

- A. Not defined—there is no explicit measurement method, and the value of AI data investment is judged mainly by subjective feel or leadership impression
- B. Scattered metrics—some metrics (e.g. scenario time-to-launch) are mentioned, but they do not form a systematic measurement method and are not agreed upon across teams
- C. Method defined—the measurement dimensions and metrics (e.g. launch cycle, data-preparation hours, rework count, accuracy) are explicitly defined and trialed and validated in at least 1-2 scenarios
- D. Operating systematically—there is a mature value-measurement framework covering dimensions such as efficiency, quality, and stability; most (ideally more than half) AI scenarios are periodically evaluated by this method and produce written reports
- N/A. Not applicable—the enterprise currently has no formally running AI scenarios and thus no subject whose value can be measured

### Q4
**Q4. In the past year, has at least one significant data-related investment had its supporting AI scenarios systematically evaluated for effectiveness using the above method, with a written or system record produced?**

- A. Never done—no formal effectiveness evaluation has been done for any data investment; once invested, it is "used and forgotten"
- B. Informal review only—there have been verbal discussions or meeting reviews, but no structured evaluation process or written record was produced
- C. Done once—in the past year, at least one significant investment was systematically evaluated with a written record produced, but the evaluation method and process have not yet been solidified into a routine mechanism
- D. Now routine—significant data investments are evaluated for effectiveness on a fixed cadence (e.g. quarterly/semi-annually), results are recorded in a queryable system, and they serve as input to subsequent decisions
- N/A. Not applicable—the enterprise had no "significant data-related investment" in the past year, so there is no subject to evaluate

## 1.3 Are Decisions Evidence-Based

### Q5
**Q5. Before deciding to launch an AI-oriented capability build or overhaul, are the business goals, expected investment, and acceptable failure conditions at least written clearly in the project charter?**

- A. No project charter—project launch lacks a formal charter document, or the charter describes only the technical solution without business goals or investment budget
- B. Partially covered—there is a charter but its content is incomplete; it usually states the business goals clearly but is vague about investment scale and exit conditions
- C. Basically clear—the charter covers the three elements (goal, investment, failure conditions), but the wording is coarse and lacks quantified exit criteria
- D. Complete and executable—all three elements are specifically quantified in the charter, exit conditions are actionable (e.g. "pause if accuracy does not reach X% within 3 months"), and it is confirmed by a review committee with a record kept
- N/A. Not applicable—the enterprise currently has no newly launched AI capability build or overhaul project

### Q6
**Q6. During project execution, are trigger points where the team needs to "stop and look at the data" pre-agreed, and when they are actually reached, is "continue, adjust, or pause" discussed based on the data?**

- A. Not preset—the project runs to completion or until a major problem occurs, with no predefined checkpoints in between
- B. Milestones but no data criteria—there are staged checkpoints (e.g. monthly reports), but the review is mainly subjective reporting, not bound to specific data metrics and trigger conditions
- C. Trigger conditions but inconsistent execution—trigger points and metrics are predefined (e.g. cost overrun of 20%, schedule slip of 2 weeks), but on reaching them the team does not always actually stop to look at the data, or lacks a written discussion record
- D. Preset and strictly executed—there are explicit trigger conditions and a corresponding decision process; when actually reached, "continue/adjust/pause" must be discussed based on the data, with a written, auditable record kept
- N/A. Not applicable—the enterprise currently has no AI-related project in progress
