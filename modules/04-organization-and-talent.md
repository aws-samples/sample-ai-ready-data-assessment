This dimension assesses whether, in its data- and AI-related work, the enterprise has formed **clear role divisions, stable collaboration processes, transferable key skills, and a decision mechanism with designated owners**, and whether AI tools are reasonably used within these processes to enhance collaboration and the quality of judgment.

## 4.1 Is the Division of Labor Process-Driven Rather Than Reliant on Individuals

### Q1
**Q1. In the end-to-end data/AI-scenario process, have the responsibility boundaries and modes of participation of each role been explicitly agreed via process documents or a RACI (responsibility-assignment matrix), etc.?**

- A. No agreement—the division of labor relies mainly on the org chart and leadership assignment; who does what is coordinated individually
- B. Rough division—teams have default conventions for the division of labor, but these are not documented, and responsibilities are often ambiguous for new hires or cross-team collaboration
- C. Process documents in place—the main processes have RACI or similar responsibility-matrix documents covering most key steps' roles and modes of participation
- D. Process-driven and continuously updated—the end-to-end process has a complete RACI definition covering all key steps, updated on a fixed cadence according to organizational changes and practical experience
- N/A. Not applicable—the enterprise's data/AI-related work is small in scale and has not yet formed an end-to-end process requiring multi-role collaboration

### Q2
**Q2. For "launch/decommission/major change" decisions on important AI scenarios, is there a fixed decision process?**

- A. No fixed process—the way decisions are made varies by person; each time a different person decides in a different way
- B. Convention but not institutionalized—the team knows "big matters need leadership," but there is no explicit review process and no defined final decision-maker
- C. Decision process in place—there is an explicit decision process (e.g. a review meeting or sign-off flow) that defines the executor and the decision-maker, and most scenarios follow it
- D. Institutionalized and traceable—the decision process is institutionalized, all major changes must go through the prescribed process, decision records are traceable, and deviations from the process are detected and explained retrospectively
- N/A. Not applicable—the enterprise currently has no AI scenarios requiring launch/decommission/major-change decisions

### Q3
**Q3. When cross-department disagreements arise on data/AI scenarios, is there a pre-agreed escalation path and decision mechanism?**

- A. Ad-hoc coordination—disagreements are resolved by ad-hoc meetings or whoever has more say, with no predefined escalation path
- B. Informal convention—everyone knows a certain executive can be asked to coordinate, but there is no formal escalation process
- C. Escalation path in place—an escalation path is defined (e.g. first intra-department coordination → then escalate to a committee → finally management), documented but executed inconsistently
- D. Institutionalized and efficient—the escalation path is institutionalized, each level has explicit response-time requirements, it runs smoothly in practice, and it prevents disagreements from lingering unresolved
- N/A. Not applicable—the enterprise's data/AI scenarios currently involve a single department, so no cross-department disagreements have arisen yet

## 4.2 Are Collaboration Processes Established Rather Than Reinvented Each Time

### Q4
**Q4. For key data assets or high-risk AI scenarios, has a periodic joint-review mechanism been set up?**

- A. No periodic review—cross-department discussions are organized only after a problem occurs
- B. Exists but irregular—reviews are organized occasionally, but there is no fixed cadence or set of participants, and they are easily crowded out by other matters
- C. Regular review in place—there are joint reviews at a fixed cadence (e.g. monthly/quarterly), attended by multiple parties such as business, data, and security, with an agenda and records
- D. Mature and improvement-driving—the periodic-review mechanism runs maturely, the action items it produces are tracked and closed out, and the review content drives continuous improvement of processes and rules
- N/A. Not applicable—the enterprise currently has no data assets / AI scenarios defined as "key" or "high-risk"

## 4.3 Is There a Clear Path for Incident and Problem Response

### Q5
**Q5. When a major problem occurs with data or an AI scenario, is there an explicit incident-response process?**

- A. Ad-hoc group chat—when a problem occurs, a group chat is hastily assembled to discuss it; who is responsible for what is unclear, and the recovery process is chaotic
- B. Rough division of labor—the team knows who is responsible for what, but there is no formal response process, and information relay and recovery steps rely on ad-hoc communication
- C. Response process in place—there is an explicit incident-response SOP (lead, information provider, recovery owner, post-mortem owner), and most incidents follow it
- D. Mature and drilled—the response process is complete and has been drilled at least once, roles are clear, response times have explicit deadlines, and every incident is followed by a post-mortem and process improvement
- N/A. Not applicable—the enterprise has not yet experienced a major data/AI-related incident
